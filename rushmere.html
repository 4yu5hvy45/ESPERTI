02 / The project

A new
frontage
for Rushmere.

Wingstop Rushmere sits within an active retail setting where the building edge has to work hard. The frontage establishes identity from the approach, while the interior continues that identity through lighting, digital ordering, graphics and material choices.

Rather than treating the build as a collection of isolated trades, the project is best understood through the interfaces: structure to roof line, brickwork to glazing, signage to fascia, and shell to customer environment.

03 / Facade + structure

The building
carries the
brand.

The facade is where structure and identity become visible together. Brickwork forms the primary field, aluminium-framed glazing opens the restaurant to the public realm, and the steel uprights create a repeated vertical datum above the roof line.

The signage then sits within the darker fascia, keeping the brand legible while preserving the architectural rhythm of the frontage.

The Wingstop identity is located within the architectural system rather than applied after completion.

05 / Interior fit-out

Inside, the
same logic
continues.

The interior uses a controlled palette rather than visual excess. Black ceilings and metalwork give the linear lighting room to read, green brand surfaces provide the colour cue, and timber-look flooring softens the customer environment. The result is unmistakably Wingstop without relying on novelty.

07 / Completed space

One project.
One language.

The finished project reads consistently from outside in. The facade gives the restaurant presence at the retail edge, the interior carries the same disciplined material language, and the customer journey is resolved through signage, glazing, lighting and clear circulation.
